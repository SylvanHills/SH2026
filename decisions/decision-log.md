## Brand Identity

| # | Date | Decision | Rationale |
|---|------|----------|-----------|
| 1 | — | **Brand purpose defined:** Sylvan Hills exists to give men a place to return to themselves. | In a world that constantly tells men who to be, the brand creates clothing that helps them remember who they are. |
| 2 | — | **Brand vision defined:** Sylvan Hills becomes the cultural signal of men who have nothing to prove and everything to express. | Rejects algorithm-driven and trend-cycle wardrobes; real style is arrived at through living. |
| 3 | — | **Design direction set:** American ease meets Italian restraint — relaxed silhouettes with disciplined tailoring. | Nothing forced, nothing flashy. |
| 4 | — | **Color palette defined:** Muted earth tones, soft neutrals, washed blacks, deep navies, warm taupes. | Colors that feel lived-in, not trendy. |
| 5 | — | **Material strategy set:** Natural, tactile, enduring materials only — cotton, wool, linen, hemp, bamboo. | Fabrics that feel better with time, not worse. |
| 6 | — | **Five core values adopted:** Refined, Maverick, Integrity, Timeless, Subtle Power. | These values remove anything unnecessary from design, language, and production. |
| 7 | — | **Sustainability folded into Integrity** — not a standalone value. | Avoids appearing performative. Sustainability is a consequence of the brand's values, not a marketing angle. |
| 8 | — | **Three brand pillars defined:** The Return (coming home to yourself), The Craft (fewer things, made better), The Life (living with intention). | Keeps Sylvan Hills coherent across all touchpoints. |
| 9 | — | **Core brand statement set:** *"Sylvan Hills is where you go when you're done performing and ready to just be."* | Anchors the emotional territory and creates differentiation. |
| 10 | — | **Product positioning line defined:** *"Clothing for the man who dresses from the inside out."* | Use for product launches, retail contexts, and press. |
| 11 | — | **Tone of voice defined:** Quietly confident, refined but not precious, intelligent without arrogance, worldly, calm, assured. | Every interaction should feel like a deep breath. |
| 12 | — | **Target customer archetypes defined:** Everett (26, creative tastemaker), Nick (35, founder/professional), Kwame (48, arrived man). | Men who dress from the inside out — not defined by trends, algorithms, or hype. |
| 13 | — | **Excluded customer explicitly defined:** Not for the man who outsources his identity to algorithms or changes wardrobe based on social media trends. | Creates a strategic boundary that protects against trend-chasing product mistakes. |

## Product

| # | Date | Decision | Rationale |
|---|------|----------|-----------|
| 14 | — | **Jackets chosen as the hero product category.** | A jacket is the first piece that sets the tone for every layer — the threshold garment from private to public. |
| 15 | — | **Hero product defined:** The Threshold Jacket — a hybrid Nehru × Chore silhouette. Working title alternatives considered: "Overlook" (childhood street name). | Nehru gives identity and distinction; chore jacket gives utility and ease. Together: "I am intentional, but grounded." |
| 16 | — | **Nehru silhouette origin:** Inspired by travel through India. Intention is to bridge cultures and bring a global mindset into the garment. | Design carries meaning, not just aesthetics. |
| 17 | — | **Usage occasion defined:** Daily uniform layer — elevated casual dinners, creative professional settings, social gatherings, weekend plans. Fills the gap between a hoodie and a blazer. | Solves the wardrobe gap men with developed taste face across contexts. |
| 18 | — | **Launch strategy: single hero product only.** No tees, no accessories, no distractions at launch. | One hero object says "This is what Sylvan Hills is." A weak first impression cannot be undone in premium menswear. |
| 19 | — | **Post-launch product roadmap set:** The Essential Tee ($75–$95) and The Foundation Trouser ($185–$240), marketed as "Complete the Look." | Introduced only after the jacket earns customer trust and defines what the brand represents. |
| 20 | Jan 2026 | **Demand validation targets set:** 30%+ positive response rate and 30–50 waitlist opt-ins before committing to manufacturing. | Validate product-market fit before capital investment. |

## Manufacturing & Production

| # | Date | Decision | Rationale |
|---|------|----------|-----------|
| 21 | Feb 2026 | **Production method: cut-and-sew manufacturing.** Blanks explicitly rejected. | The Nehru-Chore hybrid silhouette does not exist as a blank. Fit is identity. Proprietary design creates defensibility. A taste-leader customer can tell the difference between a considered garment and a relabeled commodity. |
| 22 | Feb 2026 | **Fabric strategy: natural premium fabrics** — Italian or Japanese cotton-linen blends, wool blends. | Communicates quality without logos; enables price justification at the $350–$650 retail range. |
| 23 | Feb 2026 | **Domestic factories prioritized for first run.** | Reduces communication friction and enables easier quality control for a first-time founder. |
| 24 | Feb 2026 | **Initial MOQ capped under 100 units.** | Better to sell out than sit on excess inventory. Manages capital risk. |
| 25 | Feb 2026 | **Launch with single colorway; validate demand via pre-orders or waitlist before full production.** | Reduces upfront capital requirement and inventory risk. |
| 26 | — | **Manufacturing partner in consideration:** Noble x Ready, LA. | Local, aligns with domestic-first strategy. |
| 27 | — | **Production timeline:** Partner selection and first order completed by June 10, 2026. | Align with full-time transition date; ensure production is underway at launch. |

| 29 | — | **Competitive set defined:** Officine Générale ($400–$700), Private White V.C. ($450–$800), Corridor ($300–$500). | This is where the target customer already shops. |
